UPDATED WITH NEW VERIFIED WIN/LOSS LEARNING + CURRENT CALIBRATION RULES
NEW POST-RESULT LEARNING / 120-MATCH CALIBRATION ENFORCEMENT

These rules are additive and override any earlier rule that would allow a candidate to bypass them.


1. UPDATED CALIBRATION POSITION
The current verified calibration/backtest must run AFTER the Verification Audit, Disagreement/Trap Audit, Dangerous-Underdog screen, stress tests, and
removal case, but BEFORE the final color/output. Recompute from the newest verified sample every time it changes. Never reuse stale bucket rates.


2. NO-EDGE / LOW-PROBABILITY FLOOR
- Headline or independently reconstructed favorite probability <=55%: automatic PASS/RED; never Green.

- 56-64%: cannot be Green unless the favorite defeats every major underdog pathway with at least two independent high-reliability evidence families and no
unresolved critical contradiction.

- A Red/PASS classification means "selection is not reliable enough"; it does NOT automatically mean flip to the opponent.


3. INDEPENDENT FLIP STANDARD
Never FLIP solely because of one contradictory family, recent form alone, opponent-adjustment alone, H2H alone, ranking alone, or generic external narrative.

A FLIP requires:

(a) at least 2 genuinely independent high-reliability underdog evidence families;

(b) at least 1 independent external confirmation;

(c) the underdog survives component-removal and conservative stress testing;

(d) the favorite fails the strongest evidence-based defense.

If these are not met, DOWNGRADE/PASS rather than FLIP.


4. DANGEROUS UNDERDOG CEILING OVERRIDE
For every underdog, explicitly search for a recent ceiling event:

- win over Top 10/20/50 or clearly elite/high-level opponent;

- repeated competitive performances against much stronger opposition;

- rapid ranking/Elo acceleration;

- qualifying-to-main-draw or level-jump surge;

- major same-surface breakthrough;

- recent hold/break/serve/return performance materially above long-run rating;

- comeback/deciding-set/tiebreak resilience against high-quality opposition.

A qualifying ceiling event triggers mandatory escalation. It cannot be dismissed merely because the favorite leads aggregate Elo, market, Monte Carlo, or
model vote count.


5. PRESENT-STRENGTH / RATING-LAG TEST
Compare long-run ratings against last 30/60/90-day and same-surface performance. Explicitly classify each player:

CURRENTLY REPRESENTATIVE / POSSIBLY STALE / CLEARLY LAGGING.

If the underdog's rating is lagging rapid improvement, historical Elo/ranking receives reduced decision authority.


6. ELITE-FAVORITE EXEMPTION PROHIBITED
No 80%, 85%, 90%+ favorite is exempt from the full underdog audit. High headline probability, Strong Agreement, Elite recommendation, or high Monte Carlo
cannot suppress a demonstrated dangerous-underdog signal.


7. H2H QUALITY CONTROL
H2H must be weighted for recency, surface, tour level, age/development stage, current player level, and sample size. Old/thin/non-comparable H2H cannot
veto stronger present-strength evidence.
8. CORRELATED AGREEMENT COMPRESSION
Surface Elo, General, Specialist, Monte Carlo, headline probability, and market may share inputs. Count effective independent evidence families, not raw
agreeing metrics. Strong Agreement is not sufficient for Green if effective independent evidence is low.


9. GREEN / DOUBLE-GREEN EXIT STANDARD
Before Green, the favorite must:

- defeat the underdog's 5 strongest pre-match arguments;

- survive the conservative probability stress range;

- survive removal of the strongest favorite component;

- have no unresolved Dangerous-Underdog ceiling trigger;

- have sufficient CRITICAL evidence coverage;

- survive updated historical calibration.

Double Green requires all Green conditions plus exceptionally strong independent evidence and no material unresolved contradiction.


10. POST-RESULT LEARNING LEDGER
Every completed match must be appended to the verified calibration. For every Green/Double-Green loss, classify the pre-match failure mechanism using only
evidence available before first serve. For every successful downgrade/removal and every false downgrade/flip, record why. Recurrent structures become future
downgrade or anti-overreaction rules.


VERIFICATION-SPECIFIC ADDITIONS

A. MANDATORY UNDERDOG PRESENT-STRENGTH PACKET

For every match, independently verify the underdog's last 3/5/10 matches, same-surface results, opponent quality, best recent wins, competitive losses,
ranking/Elo trajectory, serve/return trend, workload, and availability. Do this even for 85%+ favorites.

B. CEILING-EVENT SEARCH

Search specifically for the underdog's strongest recent win(s) and strongest recent performance(s), not merely average form. Identify whether these show a
level that long-run ratings have not yet absorbed.

C. FAVORITE VULNERABILITY MATCH

Take the favorite's recent loss archetypes and test whether this exact underdog has demonstrated the tools to reproduce them. Generic "favorite is stronger"
language cannot close this test.

D. STALENESS / RECENCY RECONCILIATION

When career/52-week numbers disagree with 30/60/90-day or current-surface evidence, show both and explain which deserves more weight. Do not silently
average away a rapid rise or decline.

E. CRITICAL DATA COMPLETENESS

Present strength, current surface form, serve/return, opponent quality, physical availability, and recent ceiling events are CRITICAL. A Green cannot be issued
if a critical family is genuinely unresolved unless the remaining independent evidence is overwhelming and the uncertainty is explicitly stress-tested.
        120-MATCH EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION WEIGHTING UPDATE
This section is additive and sits above the original Verification Audit. It changes evidence priority and Green/Yellow/Red gating without
deleting any existing verification requirement.

A. Empirical evidence hierarchy
Primary empirical separators: Elo separation, Matchup Closeness, Monte Carlo support, meaningful recent-form differential after
opponent adjustment, Surface Elo support with valid sample/reliability, and current market confirmation when available.
Secondary/contextual: fatigue/workload, ranking trajectory, pressure/closing, common opponents, style, straight-set call, and
recommendation labels.
Weak standalone evidence: H2H, Data Quality, Upset Risk, Strong Agreement, raw model-vote count, and one recent-form
contradiction.

B. Green gate changes
Close / coin-flip structure is a major Green blocker because the current sample was 14/27 (51.9%). Elo gap <50 is downgrade pressure
because the current sample was 32/52 (61.5%), compared with 43/53 (81.1%) at Elo gap >=50.
MC <55% is downgrade pressure (14/22, 63.6%). MC >=55% is supportive (61/83, 73.5%). MC >=70% is strong but currently
small-sample (11/12).
A Recent Form advantage >10 was 30/38 (78.9%), but Recent Form must remain opponent-adjusted and cannot independently force a
flip.
Surface Elo support >=70% was 23/28 (82.1%). Specialist support <65% was 12/22 (54.5%) and should trigger a deeper contradiction
review.
C. Independence and correlation enforcement
General Model and Specialist Model are treated as potentially highly correlated with Surface Elo/headline/MC. Agreement across these
outputs increases confidence only after independent evidence families confirm it. Do not award multiple full-strength votes for overlapping
outputs.

D. Empirical combination tests
Elo gap >=50 + MC >=55: 40/49 = 81.6%. Elo gap >=50 + recent-form advantage >10: 17/20 = 85.0%. Market >=60 + Elo gap >=50:
10/11 = 90.9% (small sample). Close + MC <55: 7/13 = 53.8%. Close + Elo gap <50: 11/21 = 52.4%.

E. Final classification changes
DOUBLE GREEN: requires strong independent-family agreement, no Close/Coin-Flip structure, successful component-removal stress
test, no dangerous-underdog ceiling trigger, and strong empirical calibration support. Small-sample combinations may support but never
create Double Green alone.
GREEN: favorite remains favored after opponent adjustment and removal of its strongest family; no multiple independent underdog
pathways; empirical structure is not in a demonstrated weak zone unless overwhelming independent evidence survives stress testing.
YELLOW: use when Elo separation is thin, MC is weak, closeness is dangerous, current form conflicts with long-run strength, or one
meaningful opponent-specific pathway remains.
RED/PASS: use when multiple independent reliable families contradict the favorite, or weak empirical structure combines with a
demonstrated underdog pathway. RED/PASS does not automatically mean FLIP.

ENFORCEMENT RULES 1-60

1. No Metric Counting Rule - 20 metrics favoring Player 1 does not automatically beat 8 favoring Player 2. Correlated statistics cannot be treated as separate
independent

votes.

2. Evidence-Family Rule - Collapse evidence into independent families before deciding: Strength/Elo, Serve, Return, Form, Surface, Opponent-Specific
Matchup,

Pressure/Closing, Workload, Tactical/Charting, Market, Availability, and Current-Tournament evidence.

3. Reliability Beats Quantity Rule - One HIGH-reliability contradiction can outweigh several LOW-reliability supporting metrics. Every decisive metric gets
HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW

reliability and FULL/REDUCED/CONTEXT/ZERO weight.

4. Sample Floor Rule - Small samples cannot receive full weight. Surface splits, H2H, tiebreaks, deciding sets, archetype results, charting, and environmental
splits need

explicit sample checks.

5. Recency x Sample Balance Rule - Do not automatically prefer recent or career data. Recent evidence is relevant but noisy; large historical samples are
stable but can be

stale. Reconcile both.

6. Opponent-Adjustment Requirement - Raw form cannot be decisive until opponent strength is considered.

7. Surface Requirement - Overall numbers cannot override strong surface-specific evidence without explanation.

8. Tour-Level Translation Rule - ITF -> Challenger -> ATP/WTA performance must be adjusted for competition level.

9. No H2H Worship Rule - Reduce H2H weight when old, on another surface, based on few meetings, or when player level/style has materially changed.

10. No Tiebreak/Clutch Worship Rule - Strong tiebreak, break-point, or deciding-set records must be tested for regression before being considered durable
skill.

11. Underlying Performance Rule - Results and scorelines must be checked against points, games, hold/break, and opponent strength.

12. Winner/Loss Quality Rule - Distinguish strong wins, weak wins, strong losses, and bad losses.

13. Scoreline Deception Rule - Never infer dominance from 2-0/3-0 alone. Check games, points, breaks, and pressure situations whenever available.

14. Current-Tournament Override Check - Current tournament serve, return, movement, workload, and conditions receive a dedicated check against historical
profile.

15. Skill-Divergence Rule - Recent Form cannot remain one number. Determine whether improvement/decline comes from serve, return, both, or
pressure-point variance.

16. Matchup Interaction Rule - Explicitly ask whether one player possesses the exact weapon that attacks the opponent's documented weakness.

17. Bidirectional Matchup Rule - Every interaction must be tested in both directions.
18. Failure-Mode Replication Rule - Identify how the favorite has actually been losing and determine whether today's opponent can reproduce those conditions.

19. Underdog Pathway Minimum Rule - Major trap concern requires one exceptionally strong pathway or multiple independent credible pathways.

20. Favorite Floor Rule - Green requires analysis of the favorite's bad-day performance, not only average or ceiling performance.

21. Underdog Ceiling Rule - Analyze the underdog's credible upper range, not only average performance.

22. Distribution Before Point Estimate Rule - Report uncertainty and performance-distribution overlap rather than relying only on headline probabilities.

23. Uncertainty Penalty Rule - Missing data, lower-tour identity uncertainty, surface transitions, small samples, and high volatility must reduce confidence.

24. Missing-Data Asymmetry Rule - Check whether missing information disproportionately affects one player. Sparse data cannot silently benefit the
better-known favorite.

25. Charting Coverage Rule - Tactical charting cannot receive full weight without reporting charted matches, surfaces, dates, and opponent quality.

26. Market Independence Rule - Market information is validation, not automatically truth. Multiple books are not automatically multiple independent signals.

37. Pre-Match Lock Rule - For live predictions, establish a cutoff timestamp. Information after first serve cannot retroactively improve the prediction.

38. Retirement/Walkover Rule - Separate normal completed matches from retirements, walkovers, and abnormal matches when calculating form/performance.

39. Injury Evidence Rule - Do not infer injury from poor performance alone. Injury requires credible public evidence.

40. Outlier Rule - One extreme match cannot dominate the conclusion. Test results with and without major outliers.

41. Sensitivity Rule - Rerun the conclusion after reducing/removing each major evidence family. If the winner flips easily, the pick is fragile.

42. Winner-Switch Rule - For reconstructed models, estimate how much Elo, hold%, break%, form, or market probability must change before the projected
winner switches.

43. Leave-One-Family-Out Rule - Remove Serve, Return, Elo, Form, Market, etc. one family at a time and test whether the conclusion survives.

44. Adversarial Underdog Rule - Intentionally construct the strongest evidence-based case for the underdog, then try to disprove it.

45. Adversarial Favorite Rule - Do the reverse for the favorite. The audit must be capable of defending whichever side survives scrutiny.

46. Contradiction Severity Rule - Classify contradictions as MINOR / MODERATE / MAJOR / CRITICAL.

47. Unresolved Contradiction Rule - A CRITICAL unresolved contradiction automatically blocks Double Green.

48. Double Green Qualification Rule - Double Green requires strong independent-family agreement, adequate samples, low distribution overlap, no major

no strong opponent-specific counter, and survival of stress testing.

49. Green Qualification Rule - Green requires the favorite to remain favored after opponent adjustment and reasonable stress tests, with no multiple

underdog pathways.

51. Automatic Red Rule - Red when multiple independent reliable evidence families contradict the Matrix favorite, or a documented favorite weakness aligns

52. PASS Rule - PASS is valid even if the Matrix says 80%. Confidence in the Matrix number and confidence in independent verification are separate.

53. No Forced Winner Rule - If evidence cannot distinguish the players reliably, say so. Verification is filtering, not manufacturing action.

54. Final Probability Reconciliation Rule - Report Matrix probability, independent reconstructed probability/range, and market no-vig probability separately.

56. Confidence Ceiling Rule - Final confidence cannot exceed the reliability of the evidence supporting it.

EXCLUDED.

VERIFICATION AUDIT

This page is part of the audit, not an optional appendix. It strengthens S. Market / Price Validation, the

Probability-Gap Alarm, Market-Veto Investigation, stress testing, and Final Green eligibility.

1. Sportsbook source minimum. For every current match, attempt at least 3 independent sportsbooks when available. Prefer

one sharp/reference or high-liquidity book plus two mainstream regulated books. An odds aggregator may help locate

prices, but it does not count as multiple independent books unless the underlying books are individually identified.

2. Same-market / same-time rule. Capture both players from the same match-winner market and the same book at

approximately the same timestamp. Record sportsbook, timestamp, favorite price, underdog price, and source URL.

Suspended, stale, one-sided, or mismatched-round prices are not valid consensus inputs.

3. De-vig each sportsbook separately. Convert both sides to raw implied probability, then compute the book-level no-vig

favorite probability as qFav / (qFav + qDog). Build the live sportsbook consensus from the median no-vig probability across

valid books; also record low/high range and dispersion.

4. Prediction markets are separate. Kalshi and other prediction markets must be recorded as a separate evidence family.

Never treat Kalshi American-style display odds as ordinary sportsbook moneylines, never blend them into sportsbook

consensus, and never use them as a substitute for a two-sided sportsbook quote.

5. Kalshi coherence check. When Kalshi is used, prefer the underlying contract price/probability and liquidity/spread

information. If its displayed American-style numbers produce an extreme synthetic overround, cannot be reconciled with

complementary contract probabilities, or materially conflict with the underlying contract price, label the display

NON-COMPARABLE / DISPLAY-STRUCTURE WARNING and give it no sportsbook-consensus weight.

6. Market integrity thresholds. A sportsbook-consensus vs Matrix-market gap of 5+ percentage points requires

investigation; 8+ pp is a MATERIAL MARKET CONFLICT; 12+ pp is a CRITICAL MARKET CONFLICT and blocks Green/Keep

until explained. Apply the same 5/8/12 pp ladder to Kalshi vs sportsbook consensus, but treat the result as a

prediction-market discrepancy rather than sportsbook evidence.

7. Book-dispersion warning. If valid sportsbook no-vig probabilities span 8+ pp, mark MARKET DISPERSION HIGH and do not

claim a precise consensus. Investigate stale lines, limits/liquidity, player identity, tournament/round, withdrawals, and news

before assigning decision weight.

8. Source-count downgrade. Three or more valid books = COMPLETE. Two valid books = PARTIAL and reduced market

weight. One valid book = SINGLE-SOURCE ONLY and cannot independently unlock Green. Zero reliable books = CURRENT

SPORTSBOOK MARKET UNAVAILABLE.

9. No market double-counting. Matrix Market Consensus, live sportsbook consensus, and prediction-market information

must remain three distinct fields. Agreement may strengthen confidence; disagreement must be explained. Do not count

multiple books as multiple independent tennis signals; they form one Market evidence family.

10. Market cannot be silently ignored. A material/critical conflict must appear in the contradiction ledger, trap score, stress

tests, removal case, and final decision rationale. If unresolved, downgrade/remove/pass rather than certifying Green.

ADDED VALIDATION RULES - CURRENT-STRENGTH & FALSE-GREEN

CONTROL

These rules are mandatory additions to the Full Verification Audit. They use only public or transparently reconstructable evidence and

are designed to prevent long-term reputation/rating strength from masking present-level uncertainty.

Current-Strength / Ranking-Lag Gate

Calculate current-strength separately from long-term ranking/Elo when recent opponent-adjusted performance materially exceeds the player's

trailing baseline. Include ranking velocity, Elo velocity where available, serve/return trajectory, quality-of-opposition progression, and recent surface

performance.

Recent Elite-Win Escalation

A recent Top-10/20/50 quality win triggers a repeatability check: underlying points/games, serve/return performance, opponent condition, surface,

and whether the performance repeated across multiple matches.

Breakthrough-Player Detection

Flag players whose last 8-12 weeks materially outperform their trailing 12-month baseline. Reweight recent opponent-adjusted evidence upward

only when supported by adequate sample and underlying performance.

Trajectory Differential

Compare both players' ranking velocity, Elo/strength velocity, opponent-adjusted form velocity, serve trajectory, return trajectory,

quality-of-opposition progression, and current-surface trajectory.

Current-Tournament Evidence Gate

Compare current-tournament serve, return, point dominance, hold/break, opponent quality, workload, conditions, and round-by-round progression.

A player with no current-condition matches receives added uncertainty rather than assumed historical form.

Long-Layoff / Rust Uncertainty

A meaningful competitive layoff widens the player's performance distribution. Historical strength may remain relevant but cannot erase uncertainty

about present floor, current conditions, and match sharpness.

Rust x Momentum Interaction

Explicitly test established/returning favorite versus match-sharp or rapidly improving opponent. Treat the interaction as a cross-feature, not two

unrelated notes.

Information-Asymmetry Penalty

When one player's present level is well observed and the other's evidence is mainly historical, reduce confidence in the less-observed player's

current estimate.
Probability Compression Under Uncertainty

When present-level uncertainty is high, widen the interval and compress extreme reconstructed probabilities toward a more conservative range.

State the reason and method.

Floor / Ceiling Revaluation

Re-estimate the favorite's realistic lower tail after layoff/inactivity and the underdog's upper tail after recent elite-quality performances. Re-run

distribution overlap.

Age x Layoff Interaction

Age alone is not a removal signal. Age combined with meaningful inactivity, absent current-condition evidence, and declining/recently volatile

performance increases uncertainty and widens the floor distribution.

Legacy / Name Bias Check

Remove player names and career accomplishments conceptually. Ask whether last 10-20 matches, surface evidence, opponent quality, trajectory,

workload, and current-condition evidence still support the same verification color.

Market-Veto Investigation

A major gap between Matrix/independent strength and a liquid market temporarily blocks Green until the discrepancy is investigated. Market is not

automatically correct, but unexplained disagreement is a mandatory contradiction.

False-Green Feedback Rule

Every Green/Double-Green loss receives a pre-match failure review. Record which public signals existed before first serve, which gates failed to

trigger, and whether the pattern should downgrade future structurally similar matches.

Pre-Match Removal Question

Before Green, answer: What is the strongest evidence that this favorite should be removed? Green requires an evidence-based explanation for

why that removal case is insufficient.

INTEGRATED FINAL DECISION ARCHITECTURE

Mandatory addition to the Verification Audit. This section controls how Verification, Disagreement, Dangerous Underdog, stress testing,

and final list eligibility interact.

1. Final Combined Decision Gate

A candidate reaches the Final Green List only if the Verification Audit approves it AND the Disagreement/Trap Audit fails to falsify it AND the

Dangerous Underdog Screen is clear AND the final stress/removal tests are passed. Failure at any required stage blocks Green.

2. Disagreement Veto Power

A RED/PASS, unresolved CRITICAL contradiction, or active Dangerous Underdog GREEN LOCK from the Disagreement Audit automatically prevents

Green regardless of the Matrix probability or Verification approval.

3. Hard Downgrade Triggers

Specified combinations must mechanically downgrade rather than merely warn. Example: rapid-riser/ranking-lag opponent + credible recent elite

performance + favorite inactivity/current-level uncertainty locks Green until escalation is passed.

4. Minimum Independent-Evidence Requirement

Do not approve from raw metric count. Green requires at least 4 genuinely independent reliable evidence families supporting the candidate after

contradictions; Double Green requires at least 6. Correlated metrics collapse into one effective family.

5. Contradiction Budget

Double Green: zero unresolved CRITICAL contradictions and no more than one unresolved MAJOR contradiction. Green: zero unresolved CRITICAL

contradictions and only limited MAJOR contradictions that are explicitly defeated. Otherwise downgrade.

6. Evidence Freshness Score

Report how much decisive evidence comes from the last 4/8/12/26 weeks and how much is older. Stale evidence cannot carry the same confidence as

fresh, surface-relevant, opponent-adjusted evidence.

7. Historical Strength vs Current Strength Split

Finish every match with separate Historical Strength and Current Estimated Strength assessments. A material divergence is a mandatory warning and

triggers deeper current-level review.
8. Probability Stress Range

Report Base, Conservative, and Optimistic reconstructed probability/range. If reasonable conservative assumptions push the favorite near 50% or flip

the winner, Green is fragile and must be downgraded unless independently resolved.

9. Removal-First Final Stage

Before approval, construct the strongest evidence-based REMOVE CASE against the Matrix winner. The candidate must defeat that case using

independent reliable evidence. Failure to defeat it blocks Green.

10. False-Green Learning Ledger

Every Green/Double-Green loss receives a pre-match failure classification using only information available before first serve: rapid riser missed, stale

rating, weak-win inflation, current decline, matchup collision, market contradiction, inactivity, etc. Recurrent structures become future downgrade

triggers.

11. Calibration by Audit Color

Track actual outcomes separately for Double Green, Green, Yellow, Red, and PASS/removed candidates. If higher colors do not materially outperform

lower colors over adequate samples, tighten thresholds.

12. Audit Confidence Is Not Win Probability

Always report Audit Confidence separately from estimated win probability. A high estimated probability with incomplete/low-quality evidence cannot

receive equally high audit confidence.

13. Minimum Coverage Threshold

Report Audit Coverage %. Double Green requires very high coverage of CRITICAL evidence families; Green requires sufficient critical coverage.

Missing supplemental metrics alone do not block approval.

14. Critical-Metric Coverage

Classify audit fields as CRITICAL / IMPORTANT / SUPPLEMENTAL. Missing current surface form, serve/return, opponent quality, availability, or

present-strength evidence matters far more than missing an obscure tactical statistic.

15. Effective Independent Evidence Count

Final output must report the number of effective independent evidence families after correlated-data compression. Do not allow dozens of correlated

metrics to create false unanimity.

16. Mandatory Strongest-Underdog Table

For every proposed Green, list the underdog's 5 strongest pre-match arguments, reliability/sample, evidence family, and whether each was

DEFEATED / UNRESOLVED / SUPPORTS REMOVAL.

17. Final Rejection Reason

Every removed/downgraded favorite receives one concise human-readable reason identifying the decisive structural risk rather than merely listing

metrics.
