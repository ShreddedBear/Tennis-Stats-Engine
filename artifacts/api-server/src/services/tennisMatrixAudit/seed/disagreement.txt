                       TENNIS MATRIX — DISAGREEMENT / TRAP AUDIT
UPDATED WITH NEW VERIFIED WIN/LOSS LEARNING + CURRENT CALIBRATION RULES
NEW POST-RESULT LEARNING / 120-MATCH CALIBRATION ENFORCEMENT

These rules are additive and override any earlier rule that would allow a candidate to bypass them.


1. UPDATED CALIBRATION POSITION
The current verified calibration/backtest must run AFTER the Verification Audit, Disagreement/Trap Audit, Dangerous-Underdog screen, stress tests, and
removal case, but BEFORE the final color/output. Recompute from the newest verified sample every time it changes. Never reuse stale bucket rates.


2. NO-EDGE / LOW-PROBABILITY FLOOR
- Headline or independently reconstructed favorite probability <=55%: automatic PASS/RED; never Green.

- 56-64%: cannot be Green unless the favorite defeats every major underdog pathway with at least two independent high-reliability evidence families and no
unresolved critical contradiction.

- A Red/PASS classification means "selection is not reliable enough"; it does NOT automatically mean flip to the opponent.


3. INDEPENDENT FLIP STANDARD
Never FLIP solely because of one contradictory family, recent form alone, opponent-adjustment alone, H2H alone, ranking alone, or generic external narrative.

A FLIP requires:

(a) at least 2 genuinely independent high-reliability underdog evidence families;

(b) at least 1 independent external confirmation;

(c) the underdog survives component-removal and conservative stress testing;

(d) the favorite fails the strongest evidence-based defense.

If these are not met, DOWNGRADE/PASS rather than FLIP.


4. DANGEROUS UNDERDOG CEILING OVERRIDE
For every underdog, explicitly search for a recent ceiling event:

- win over Top 10/20/50 or clearly elite/high-level opponent;

- repeated competitive performances against much stronger opposition;

- rapid ranking/Elo acceleration;

- qualifying-to-main-draw or level-jump surge;

- major same-surface breakthrough;

- recent hold/break/serve/return performance materially above long-run rating;

- comeback/deciding-set/tiebreak resilience against high-quality opposition.

A qualifying ceiling event triggers mandatory escalation. It cannot be dismissed merely because the favorite leads aggregate Elo, market, Monte Carlo, or
model vote count.


5. PRESENT-STRENGTH / RATING-LAG TEST
Compare long-run ratings against last 30/60/90-day and same-surface performance. Explicitly classify each player:

CURRENTLY REPRESENTATIVE / POSSIBLY STALE / CLEARLY LAGGING.

If the underdog's rating is lagging rapid improvement, historical Elo/ranking receives reduced decision authority.


6. ELITE-FAVORITE EXEMPTION PROHIBITED
No 80%, 85%, 90%+ favorite is exempt from the full underdog audit. High headline probability, Strong Agreement, Elite recommendation, or high Monte Carlo
cannot suppress a demonstrated dangerous-underdog signal.


7. H2H QUALITY CONTROL
H2H must be weighted for recency, surface, tour level, age/development stage, current player level, and sample size. Old/thin/non-comparable H2H cannot
veto stronger present-strength evidence.
8. CORRELATED AGREEMENT COMPRESSION
Surface Elo, General, Specialist, Monte Carlo, headline probability, and market may share inputs. Count effective independent evidence families, not raw
agreeing metrics. Strong Agreement is not sufficient for Green if effective independent evidence is low.


9. GREEN / DOUBLE-GREEN EXIT STANDARD
Before Green, the favorite must:

- defeat the underdog's 5 strongest pre-match arguments;

- survive the conservative probability stress range;

- survive removal of the strongest favorite component;

- have no unresolved Dangerous-Underdog ceiling trigger;

- have sufficient CRITICAL evidence coverage;

- survive updated historical calibration.

Double Green requires all Green conditions plus exceptionally strong independent evidence and no material unresolved contradiction.


10. POST-RESULT LEARNING LEDGER
Every completed match must be appended to the verified calibration. For every Green/Double-Green loss, classify the pre-match failure mechanism using only
evidence available before first serve. For every successful downgrade/removal and every false downgrade/flip, record why. Recurrent structures become future
downgrade or anti-overreaction rules.


DISAGREEMENT-SPECIFIC ADDITIONS

A. FALSE-POSITIVE TRAP DEFENSE

For every proposed downgrade/flip, construct the strongest case that the apparent disagreement is noise/correlation/stale evidence. If that defense survives,
do not flip.

B. DISAGREEMENT QUALITY > DISAGREEMENT COUNT

Report each contradiction as INDEPENDENT / PARTIALLY CORRELATED / DUPLICATIVE and HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW reliability. A pile of correlated
disagreement cannot outweigh multiple independent favorite families.

C. FLIP VS PASS SEPARATION

The audit must output KEEP / DOWNGRADE / REMOVE-PASS / FLIP separately. Never convert "favorite unsafe" into "underdog is the pick" without satisfying
the Independent Flip Standard.

D. RECENT-FORM OVERREACTION GUARD

Recent form and opponent adjustment can create CAUTION or removal, but cannot independently force a flip when Elo, serve/return, market, and other
independent evidence remain materially opposite.

E. CEILING-EVENT HANDOFF

Any verified underdog ceiling event must be carried verbatim into the final trap packet and stress test. The final gate must explicitly state why the favorite still
survives it, or Green is blocked.
         120-MATCH EMPIRICAL DISAGREEMENT / TRAP OVERLAY
This section is additive. It updates how the existing Disagreement/Trap Audit interprets contradictions; it does not delete the original audit.

A. Governing empirical rule
Disagreement QUALITY outranks disagreement COUNT. A contradiction becomes decision-relevant only after checking independence,
reliability, sample, magnitude, recency, surface relevance, and opponent adjustment. Raw vote count is not a Green or Red rule.

B. Mandatory high-risk structures
Close / coin-flip structure: current 120-match record was 14/27 (51.9%). Treat this as a major trap zone. Green requires unusually
strong independent evidence and successful stress testing.
Elo separation under 50 points: current record was 32/52 (61.5%) versus 43/53 (81.1%) when the Matrix pick held an Elo gap of at
least 50. A sub-50 gap is a material disagreement/fragility flag, not an automatic flip.
Monte Carlo below 55%: current record was 14/22 (63.6%). This is downgrade pressure. MC >=70% was 11/12, but that sample is too
small to function as a standalone Green rule.
Specialist support below 65%: current record was 12/22 (54.5%). This becomes a major warning when the headline probability is
materially higher.

C. Correlated-agreement penalty
General Model, Specialist Model, Surface Elo, headline probability, Monte Carlo, and Market may share inputs. Do not count them as
independent simply because they all agree. Strong Model Agreement is contextual only; it cannot rescue a weak independent-family
structure.

D. Metrics demoted to contextual status
Data Quality, Upset Risk, Recommendation label, raw disagreement count, fatigue alone, H2H alone, and a single Recent Form
contradiction are not standalone downgrade/flip triggers. They remain reportable context.
E. Required trap decision sequence
1. Check Matchup Closeness and headline WP burden zone. 2. Measure Elo separation and Surface Elo support with reliability/sample.
3. Compare MC support and headline-vs-MC gap. 4. Test recent-form differential, but require opponent adjustment. 5. Search for
dangerous-underdog ceiling events and demonstrated matchup pathways. 6. Separate independent evidence from correlated Matrix
outputs. 7. Remove the favorite's strongest family and rerun. 8. Output KEEP / DOWNGRADE / REMOVE-PASS / FLIP separately.

F. Empirical combinations
Elo gap >=50 + MC >=55: 40/49 = 81.6%. Elo gap >=50 + recent-form advantage >10: 17/20 = 85.0%. Close + MC <55: 7/13 = 53.8%.
Close + Elo gap <50: 11/21 = 52.4%. These combinations are calibration overlays, not permanent laws.

G. Green-blocking rule added
A candidate cannot receive Green solely because the Matrix headline, Recommendation, Strong Agreement, General Model, Specialist
Model, and Surface Elo all point the same way. Green requires independent-family support and must survive the underdog ceiling,
present-strength, matchup, and component-removal tests.

Reading the finished audit

- A high disagreement count does not automatically mean "flip the pick" — it means the pick is surviving real pushback in the data, which

is more useful to know than a clean confidence score alone.
- Zero-disagreement matches are the only ones where nothing in the dataset pushes back at all — still not a guarantee, just the cleanest

read available.

- This document deliberately isolates the opposing case only. Pair it with a separate confirming-evidence pass

(component-by-component P1 vs P2 differential, contradiction check, final Green/Yellow/Red) rather than using disagreement count

alone as the verdict.

- State plainly if either the independent Green-list cross-reference source or full external (live) validation wasn't available/run for a given

batch — don't drop those steps silently.

PART 2 — PREDICTED-LOSER / TRAP-GAME DETECTION (same

extensiveness as the original verification audit, run in the opposite direction)

Purpose: Part 1 asks "what disagrees with the favorite." Part 2 builds the strongest honest case *for the underdog* — the player the

Matrix predicts to lose — using the same full metric-family sweep as a full verification audit, just pointed at the other player. A "trap

game" is a match where the underdog's case is unusually strong even though the Matrix still favors the other player.

Same rule as Part 1: nothing invented. Every line traces to a real field. Mark UNAVAILABLE where the data doesn't exist rather than

guessing.

refreshed each time)

Findings from the sample run on file (100 matches, 30 upsets) — treat as a prior to test against future data, not a permanent

law:

- The Matrix's own "Upset Risk" flag caught almost none of the actual upsets — 29 of 30 upsets were labeled "Low" upset risk
beforehand. Do not use the Matrix's self-flag alone to rule a match safe from being a trap game.

- Data Quality was statistically identical between upsets and correct picks (~80% either way). Thin data is not what predicts upsets in

this sample — don't lean on DQ as a trap-game filter.

- "Moderate lean" produced the *most* upsets in raw count (57% of all upsets) even though "Close matchup" had the *highest rate* (50%

vs 27%). A trap-game scan has to cover the full "Moderate lean" pool, not just matches literally labeled a coin flip — most

upsets by volume come from there simply because there are more of those matches.

- "High Confidence" recommendation carried the single highest upset rate of any label (41.7%) — higher than "Moderate Confidence"

(30%) and "Elite" (27%). Treat "High Confidence" as a caution flag, not reassurance, consistent with Part 1.

- Monte Carlo support under 55% for the favorite showed up in 26.7% of upsets vs 18.6% of correct picks — a real but modest lean; not a

standalone trigger by itself.

- Monte Carlo never actually dropped below 50% for the stated favorite anywhere in the sample tested. Any live match where it does is

outside the historical envelope — flag it as higher-uncertainty than anything the backtest can vouch for, not lower.

2.2 — Underdog case-building: full metric-family sweep

For every match, run the underdog through the same families a full verification audit would run the favorite through. Mark each FOR

(helps the underdog), AGAINST (helps the favorite), or UNAVAILABLE.

Matrix Headline & Confidence

- Headline WP margin — how close to 50% is it really?

- Recommendation label — is it one of the historically weak labels (see 2.1)?

- Upset Risk as self-flagged — note it, but weight it low per the finding above.

- Model Agreement label — "Strong" does not reliably rule out an upset (see 2.1); check anyway for what it's worth.

- Data Quality — note it, but don't treat low DQ as the reason to expect an upset, or high DQ as protection.

Surface / Elo

- Underdog's Surface Elo raw probability and reliability/sample — is it actually close to the favorite's once you set aside the blend

weighting?

- Underdog's Elo trend — improving, stable, or declining independent of the raw number.

- Surface specialization label — is the underdog the surface specialist here even though they're not the overall favorite?

Serve / Return

- Underdog's Serve/Return raw probability and reliability.

- Does the underdog's return profile specifically attack the favorite's serve profile (style/matchup fit), separate from the raw number?

Recent Form

- Underdog's Recent Form raw probability, trend direction, and reliability.

- Any explicit "improving" trend flag on the underdog, especially paired with a "declining" or "stable" flag on the favorite.

- Opponent-adjusted form for the underdog, if present — are their recent results against tougher or weaker competition than the raw form

score implies?

H2H

- Raw H2H number and reliability/sample. In most Matrix exports seen so far this is near-zero and excluded — say so plainly rather than

treating a 50/50 H2H number as a real signal either way.

Market

- Does the market component favor the underdog, or at least sit closer to 50/50 than the headline WP? A market that disagrees with (or is

much less confident than) the model's own headline is one of the more credible trap-game signals, since market data aggregates

outside information the Matrix's other components may not have.

General / Specialist Model

- Underdog's raw probability on each, and reliability/sample/status (Active vs Excluded, Low vs High availability). A specialist model

favoring the underdog on a thin sample is weaker evidence than the same signal on a deep one — say which it is.
Fatigue / Physical

- Underdog's fatigue index vs the favorite's. A fresher underdog against a fatigued favorite is one of the more concrete, checkable

trap-game signals available in this data.

- Matches played in the trailing window for both, if present.

Monte Carlo / Set Score

- Underdog's implied probability (100 − favorite's MC%).

- Is the favorite's MC support under 55% (modest historical upset lean) or under 50% (numerically favors the underdog, outside the

historical sample entirely — flag as high-uncertainty)?

- How far does the simulation range extend in the underdog's favor?

- Does the predicted set score (e.g., 2-0) actually match what the simulation supports, or is a 2-1/underdog-competitive outcome more

consistent with the range?

Style / Matchup

- Style labels for both players (e.g., all-court, clay specialist, big server) — does the underdog's style specifically counter the favorite's,

independent of the surface Elo number?

Ranking / External

- UNAVAILABLE unless a dedicated external-validation pass has actually been run (see Part 1's completion-check note) — do not infer

ranking trajectory or current form from Matrix fields that don't contain it.

PART 3 — FULL PREDICTED-LOSER VERIFICATION AUDIT (Mirror of the

Winner Verification Audit)

Purpose: This is the anti-favorite version of the full verification audit. The goal is not merely to find disagreement with the Matrix pick.

The goal is to determine whether the player predicted to lose has a *real, repeatable, opponent-specific pathway to winning* that the

favorite-side model may be underweighting.

For every matchup, treat the predicted loser as a candidate winner and run the entire verification framework in reverse. Do not stop after

finding one attractive underdog statistic. The underdog must survive the same reliability, sample-size, opponent-adjustment,

historical-validation, and external-validation standards as a projected winner.

3.1 — Mandatory evidence classification

Every item evaluated for the predicted loser must receive one of these labels:

- UD+ STRONG: materially supports the predicted loser, with reliable sample/relevance.

- UD+ WEAK: supports the predicted loser but sample/reliability/relevance is limited.

- FAV+ STRONG: materially supports the favorite and directly weakens the trap-game case.

- FAV+ WEAK: mildly supports the favorite.

- CONFLICT: credible evidence exists on both sides.

Page 7

- UNAVAILABLE: no trustworthy data supplied/found.

- EXCLUDED: field exists but should carry zero decision weight because the Matrix itself excludes it or reliability is effectively unusable.

Do not count UD+ WEAK the same as UD+ STRONG. Do not count five correlated metrics as five independent signals.

PART 15 — PRIMARY OPERATING PRINCIPLE

The disagreement audit is not finished when it proves that the favorite has weaknesses. It is finished only when it answers both

questions:

1. Why can the favorite lose?

2. Does this specific underdog possess the actual weapons, form, physical condition, matchup fit, market support, and

historical profile required to make those failure modes happen today?

A true trap game requires both sides of that equation. The goal is to detect the predicted loser *before the upset happens*, without

turning every imperfect favorite into a fake trap signal.

ADDED DISAGREEMENT RULES - FALSE-FAVORITE ESCALATION

These rules are mandatory additions to the Disagreement/Trap Audit. Their purpose is to force removal or downgrade review when a

historically strong favorite faces a rapidly improving, match-sharp, or under-ranked opponent.

FALSE-FAVORITE ESCALATION GATE - GREEN LOCK

If the favorite has meaningful inactivity or no current-condition evidence AND the opponent shows rapid improvement, ranking lag, recent elite wins,

or unusually strong opponent-adjusted form, GREEN and DOUBLE GREEN are LOCKED until the escalation below is completed.

1. Current-Strength Revaluation

Estimate both players' present strength separately from career/name value using recent opponent-adjusted results, surface performance,

serve/return trajectory, ranking/Elo velocity, and quality of opposition.

2. Rapid-Riser / Ranking-Lag Override

If the underdog's recent level is materially ahead of ranking/Elo, long-term ratings cannot dismiss the underdog. Quantify the gap between trailing

baseline and recent current-strength estimate.

3. Recent Elite-Win Escalation

Investigate recent high-quality wins for repeatability using underlying points/games, serve/return, opponent quality, surface, and follow-up

performance.

4. Favorite Rust / Layoff Penalty

Meaningful inactivity widens the favorite's floor and blocks clean confidence based primarily on historical strength. Compare layoff length, match

sharpness, current-surface adaptation, and first-match-back history when reconstructable.

5. No Current-Tournament Evidence Penalty

If the underdog has current-condition evidence and the favorite has none, increase favorite uncertainty and require affirmative current-strength

support before Green.

6. Rust x Momentum Cross-Feature

Directly test returning established favorite versus match-sharp / rapidly improving opponent. This interaction can create trap risk even when each

factor alone is moderate.
7. Underdog Ceiling Revaluation

Recent elite-quality performances update the underdog's realistic upper range. Recalculate favorite-floor vs underdog-ceiling overlap.

8. Favorite Floor Revaluation

Layoff, volatility, uncertain current condition, or recent decline widens the favorite's lower tail. Recalculate distribution overlap.

9. Information-Asymmetry Penalty

If present-level evidence is much richer for the underdog than the favorite, do not let historical certainty substitute for current evidence.

10. Legacy / Name Bias Test

Ignore names, titles, reputation, and career accomplishments. Re-evaluate using recent 10-20 matches, surface, opponent quality, serve/return

trajectory, workload, and current-condition evidence. If the color changes, flag LEGACY/NAME BIAS RISK.

11. Market-Veto Investigation

Large unexplained market disagreement blocks Green. Investigate line level/movement, source quality, current availability news, and whether the

market is pricing a present-level risk the historical metrics miss.

12. Probability Compression

When present-level uncertainty is high, reject extreme certainty. Widen the probability interval and test whether the pick remains favorable under

conservative current-strength assumptions.

13. Mandatory Removal Question

Before any Green list is finalized, answer: What is the strongest evidence that this favorite should be REMOVED? If the audit cannot convincingly

defeat that case with independent reliable evidence, remove or downgrade the favorite.

14. Adversarial Breakthrough Case

Construct the strongest evidence-based case that the improving underdog is already better than the long-term rating suggests. Test it against

surface, opponent adjustment, serve/return, current tournament, market, and sample reliability.

15. Green Burden-of-Proof Rule

In an escalation match, absence of proof for the underdog is not proof for the favorite. The favorite must affirmatively demonstrate current

superiority after all adjustments.

16. False-Green Historical Feedback

Every Green/Double-Green loss becomes a structural failure case. Store the pre-match warning configuration and use it as a future downgrade

trigger when the same pattern recurs.

17. No Future Leakage

Only pre-match information may explain whether the pick should have been removed. In-match illness, vomiting, injury, medical timeout, or

performance cannot be used retroactively unless publicly known before first serve.

18. Escalation Exit Standard

GREEN unlocks only if the favorite survives current-strength revaluation, trajectory differential, floor/ceiling overlap, market investigation,

adversarial underdog case, and removal-question review. Otherwise downgrade to YELLOW/RED/PASS as supported.

4. Ranking Velocity + Acceleration - Measure ranking change and whether the rate of improvement/decline itself is accelerating.

5. Opponent-Quality Acceleration - Test whether recent performance is occurring against progressively stronger opposition.

6. Upset Capability Index - Measure recent performance against Top 10/20/50/100, higher-rated opponents, and betting favorites where historical

pricing is available.

7. Upset Recency Index - Weight recent high-quality upsets more heavily than old upsets while preserving sample reliability.

8. Near-Upset Index - Capture competitive losses to elite opponents using sets, tiebreaks, game/point dominance, break differential, and closeness.

9. Hidden Improvement Index - Detect rising point%, hold%, break%, second-serve, return, and opponent-adjusted performance even when W/L

remains mediocre.

10. Quality-of-Loss Trend - Determine whether recent losses are increasingly restricted to stronger opponents and whether underlying performance in

those losses is improving.

11. Favorite Bad-Loss Frequency - Count losses to materially lower-ranked/lower-rated opponents over multiple recency windows and on the relevant

surface.

12. Favorite Escape Frequency - Count wins where the favorite survived despite weak underlying point/game/break performance or repeated pressure

exposure.

13. Expected-Wins vs Actual-Wins Gap - Compare recent actual W/L with expected results from transparent pre-match strength or point/game

performance estimates.

14. Opponent-Adjusted Point Dominance Trend - Track point-level dominance after opponent-strength adjustment and compare trend direction for

both players.

15. Serve Residual Trend - Actual serve performance minus expected serve performance against the returners faced.

16. Return Residual Trend - Actual return performance minus expected return performance against the servers faced.

17. Second-Serve Survival Trend - Track second-serve points won and exposure after opponent adjustment, especially against aggressive return

profiles.
18. Break Creation Trend - Track break-point opportunities generated per return game and its recent/surface/opponent-adjusted trajectory.

19. Break Prevention Trend - Track break-point opportunities conceded per service game and trajectory.

20. Pressure Opportunity Differential - Compare how frequently each player creates and faces high-leverage break situations, not merely conversion

percentages.

21. Match-Control Without Scoreline - Reconstruct control from points, service points, return points, breaks, and break opportunities while deliberately

ignoring the final result.

22. Performance Stability vs Strong Opposition - Measure whether performance deteriorates sharply or remains stable as opponent quality increases.

23. Opponent Strength Elasticity - Estimate how much point/serve/return performance changes as opponent strength rises.

24. Surface Strength Elasticity - Estimate whether the matchup gap expands or compresses on today's surface relative to overall performance.

25. Favorite Advantage Compression - Measure how much the favorite's edge shrinks through overall -> surface -> recent -> opponent-adjusted ->

current-tournament filters.

26. Underdog Threat Expansion - Measure whether the underdog's estimated strength rises as stale/irrelevant historical information is removed.

27. Peak-Recent-Performance Frequency - Count how often the underdog has actually reached an elite or matchup-winning performance band

recently.

28. Ceiling Repeatability - Distinguish one outlier peak from repeated high-level performances over the recent sample.

29. Performance Change-Point Detection - Use transparent statistical change-point or segmented-trend methods where sample permits to identify

meaningful shifts in current level.

30. EWMA Current Form - Exponentially weight recent point/game/serve/return performance so the newest evidence matters more without discarding

older observations.

31. Separate Serve EWMA and Return EWMA - Maintain independent exponentially weighted serve and return trajectories.

32. Current-Level Confidence Interval - Every reconstructed current-strength estimate must include uncertainty/range and sample basis.

33. Probability Range Instead of Single Probability - Report a plausible reconstructed probability interval, not only a single point estimate.

34. Worst-Reasonable-Case Simulation - Stress-test lower-bound favorite assumptions against upper-bound underdog assumptions. If modest

changes flip the winner, Green is fragile.

35. Monte Carlo Parameter Uncertainty - Sample plausible serve/return/hold parameters from uncertainty ranges instead of fixing one estimate.

36. Upset Monte Carlo - Estimate how often plausible favorite-floor/underdog-ceiling combinations produce the upset.

37. Path-to-Upset Simulation - Estimate plausible upset routes: straight sets, decider, break-heavy, tiebreak, second-serve attack, closing failure, etc.

38. Multiple Independent Upset Routes - Count independent credible pathways by which the underdog can win. Multiple routes materially increase trap

risk.

39. Network Strength Rating - Build a transparent player-opponent graph from recent public results to identify players whose ranking may lag their

competitive network position.

40. Temporal Network Rating - Recency-weight the network so current opponent relationships matter more than stale ones.

41. Second-Degree Opponent Strength - Evaluate not only who a player beat, but the recent strength/results of those opponents' opponents.

42. Quality-Win Network Density - Test whether strong results form a connected pattern against genuinely strong competition rather than isolated

anomalies.

43. Common-Opponent Recency - Weight recent same-surface common opponents more heavily than old comparisons.

44. Common-Opponent Performance Residual - Compare each player's performance against shared opponents relative to expected performance.

45. Archetype-Neighborhood Analysis - Find statistically similar recent opponents using observable surface, strength, serve/return, rally, and style

variables rather than broad labels alone.

46. Nearest-Opponent Match Search - Construct a transparent similarity vector and retrieve the closest historical opponent profiles for each player.

47. Favorite Failure Twins - Find past favorite losses/struggles against opponents most similar to today's underdog.

48. Underdog Success Twins - Find underdog wins/near-wins against opponents most similar to today's favorite.

49. Twin Outcome Rate - Calculate outcomes across the comparable-match sample instead of relying on anecdotal similarities.

50. Twin Reliability Gate - No historical-twin conclusion without sample size, similarity strength, recency, and surface relevance.

51. Superstar / Name-Value Guard - Automatically escalate historically elite favorites when the opponent's current level is rising materially faster than
ranking recognition.

52. Career-vs-Current Strength Divergence - Quantify the gap between long-term/career strength and current reconstructed strength for both players.

53. Historical Reputation Contribution - Recalculate the favorite's advantage after excluding evidence older than 12 months where possible; report how

much the edge changes.

54. Favorite Recognition Bias Alarm - If career evidence strongly favors the favorite but recent/surface/opponent-adjusted evidence is close, force

downgrade review.

55. Freshness Asymmetry Alarm - If most favorite-supporting evidence is old while underdog-supporting evidence is recent, lock Green pending

escalation.

56. Current Evidence Coverage Ratio - Report what percentage of decision evidence comes from last 4/8/12/26 weeks.

57. Independent Evidence Effective Count - Reduce correlated statistics into effective independent evidence families rather than counting every metric

as a separate vote.

58. Contradiction Concentration - Distinguish many contradictions from one correlated family versus contradictions across multiple independent

families.

59. Underdog Evidence Diversity Score - Count and weight independent evidence families supporting the upset case.

60. Favorite Evidence Diversity Score - Require comparable diversity in the favorite's case before Green.

61. Break-After-Break Momentum Profile - From game/point logs, reconstruct break consolidation and immediate rebreak patterns for both players.

62. Interruption Sensitivity Proxy - Where logs and public interruption data support it, compare observable performance before/after delays or

interruptions; do not infer psychology.

63. Error Pressure Profile - When charting exists, distinguish winners, forced errors, unforced errors, and double faults under opponent pressure.

64. Error Spike Vulnerability - Identify whether favorite losses repeatedly contain abrupt double-fault/unforced-error deterioration under return pressure.

65. Underdog Error-Induction Ability - Determine whether the underdog repeatedly causes stronger opponents to produce worse serve/shot outcomes

than their baselines.

66. Match-State Resilience - Reconstruct performance after being broken, losing a set, failing to serve out a set, or losing a tiebreak.

67. Favorite Shock Response - Specifically test favorite performance after losing the first break/set against lower-rated opposition.

68. Underdog Lead Protection vs Better Players - Measure whether the underdog can consolidate and close leads against higher-rated opponents.

69. Upset Conversion Rate - Among matches where the underdog reaches a break/set lead against materially stronger players, measure how often the

upset is completed.

70. Dangerous-Underdog Gate - If rapid-riser/ranking-lag + current-strength improvement + credible elite-opponent performance trigger together, Green

is unavailable until the Dangerous Underdog Screen is completed.

MANDATORY EXIT TEST

The favorite cannot unlock GREEN merely because it remains the higher-ranked or historically stronger player. GREEN unlocks

only after the audit defeats the strongest removal case using multiple independent, reliable, current, surface-relevant,

opponent-adjusted evidence families and the conclusion survives uncertainty/sensitivity testing.

DATA-COVERAGE RULE

When advanced point/shot/charting data are unavailable for a player, mark the affected metric PARTIAL or UNAVAILABLE and continue. Missing

charting data must increase uncertainty; it must never be replaced with invented observations or silently interpreted as evidence for the favorite.

INTEGRATED VETO & HANDOFF ARCHITECTURE

Mandatory addition to the Disagreement/Trap Audit. This section gives the disagreement system explicit veto/handoff authority inside the

combined workflow.

1. Final Combined Decision Gate

A candidate reaches the Final Green List only if the Verification Audit approves it AND the Disagreement/Trap Audit fails to falsify it AND the

Dangerous Underdog Screen is clear AND the final stress/removal tests are passed. Failure at any required stage blocks Green.

2. Disagreement Veto Power

A RED/PASS, unresolved CRITICAL contradiction, or active Dangerous Underdog GREEN LOCK from the Disagreement Audit automatically prevents

Green regardless of the Matrix probability or Verification approval.
3. Hard Downgrade Triggers

Specified combinations must mechanically downgrade rather than merely warn. Example: rapid-riser/ranking-lag opponent + credible recent elite

performance + favorite inactivity/current-level uncertainty locks Green until escalation is passed.

4. Minimum Independent-Evidence Requirement

Do not approve from raw metric count. Green requires at least 4 genuinely independent reliable evidence families supporting the candidate after

contradictions; Double Green requires at least 6. Correlated metrics collapse into one effective family.

5. Contradiction Budget

Double Green: zero unresolved CRITICAL contradictions and no more than one unresolved MAJOR contradiction. Green: zero unresolved CRITICAL

contradictions and only limited MAJOR contradictions that are explicitly defeated. Otherwise downgrade.

6. Evidence Freshness Score

Report how much decisive evidence comes from the last 4/8/12/26 weeks and how much is older. Stale evidence cannot carry the same confidence as

fresh, surface-relevant, opponent-adjusted evidence.

7. Historical Strength vs Current Strength Split

Finish every match with separate Historical Strength and Current Estimated Strength assessments. A material divergence is a mandatory warning and

triggers deeper current-level review.

8. Probability Stress Range

Report Base, Conservative, and Optimistic reconstructed probability/range. If reasonable conservative assumptions push the favorite near 50% or flip

the winner, Green is fragile and must be downgraded unless independently resolved.

9. Removal-First Final Stage

Before approval, construct the strongest evidence-based REMOVE CASE against the Matrix winner. The candidate must defeat that case using

independent reliable evidence. Failure to defeat it blocks Green.

10. False-Green Learning Ledger

Every Green/Double-Green loss receives a pre-match failure classification using only information available before first serve: rapid riser missed, stale

rating, weak-win inflation, current decline, matchup collision, market contradiction, inactivity, etc. Recurrent structures become future downgrade

triggers.

11. Calibration by Audit Color

Track actual outcomes separately for Double Green, Green, Yellow, Red, and PASS/removed candidates. If higher colors do not materially outperform

lower colors over adequate samples, tighten thresholds.

12. Audit Confidence Is Not Win Probability

Always report Audit Confidence separately from estimated win probability. A high estimated probability with incomplete/low-quality evidence cannot

receive equally high audit confidence.

13. Minimum Coverage Threshold

Report Audit Coverage %. Double Green requires very high coverage of CRITICAL evidence families; Green requires sufficient critical coverage.

Missing supplemental metrics alone do not block approval.

14. Critical-Metric Coverage

Classify audit fields as CRITICAL / IMPORTANT / SUPPLEMENTAL. Missing current surface form, serve/return, opponent quality, availability, or

present-strength evidence matters far more than missing an obscure tactical statistic.

15. Effective Independent Evidence Count

Final output must report the number of effective independent evidence families after correlated-data compression. Do not allow dozens of correlated

metrics to create false unanimity.

16. Mandatory Strongest-Underdog Table

For every proposed Green, list the underdog's 5 strongest pre-match arguments, reliability/sample, evidence family, and whether each was

DEFEATED / UNRESOLVED / SUPPORTS REMOVAL.

17. Final Rejection Reason

Every removed/downgraded favorite receives one concise human-readable reason identifying the decisive structural risk rather than merely listing
metrics.

Disagreement-Specific Handoff Rule

The Disagreement Audit must explicitly return one of: CLEAR / CAUTION / GREEN LOCK / RED VETO / PASS VETO. CLEAR permits progression;

CAUTION requires final stress review; GREEN LOCK/RED VETO/PASS VETO blocks Green.

Dangerous Underdog Handoff Rule

A 3-of-5 strong Dangerous Underdog trigger cannot be ignored by the combined decision gate. The favorite must pass the full escalation exit standard

before the lock can be cleared.

FINAL INTEGRATED WORKFLOW

MANDATORY WORKFLOW: Tennis Matrix Candidate -> Verification Audit -> Disagreement/Trap Audit -> Dangerous Underdog Screen ->

Stress Test -> Removal Case -> FINAL GREEN LIST

No candidate may bypass a stage. A blocked stage must be resolved or the candidate remains downgraded/removed. The purpose is filtering, not

forcing a winner.
