SCOPE RULE
This edition contains only metrics that can be obtained from reliable public sources or transparently reconstructed
from public observations. Private/proprietary model internals, unsupplied private datasets, and non-public
physiological measurements have been removed.


Evidence labels: DIRECT • RECONSTRUCTED • PARTIAL • UNAVAILABLE • EXCLUDED



1. Surface Strength

• Surface Elo: a player's Elo rating calculated specifically for the surface being played on.
• Elo Win Probability: the win probability implied purely by the Elo rating differential between the two players.
• Surface Sample Depth: the number of matches available to calculate a player's surface-specific rating.
• Effective Weighted Sample: the sample size after applying recency and quality weighting rather than a raw count.
• Surface Elo Trend/Momentum: the recent direction of change in a player's surface-specific Elo rating.
• Peak Elo vs Current Elo: the gap between a player's highest-ever Elo and their present rating.
• Hard-Court Record: a player's overall win-loss record on hard courts.
• Last-52-Week Hard-Court Record: a player's hard-court win-loss record limited to the past year.

2. Serve Profile

• Hold %: the percentage of a player's own service games that they win.
• Service Points Won %: the percentage of all points won while serving.
• First-Serve-In %: the percentage of first serves landed in play.
• First-Serve Points Won %: the percentage of points won when the first serve goes in.
• Second-Serve Points Won %: the percentage of points won when the player must hit a second serve.
• Ace Rate: the percentage of service points won outright with an unreturned serve.
• Double-Fault Rate: the percentage of service points lost due to a double fault.
• Break Points Saved %: the percentage of break points against the player that they successfully defend.
• Service Games Held: the count of service games won without being broken.
• Serve-Out Reliability: how consistently a player successfully closes out a set or match while serving for it.

3. Return Profile

• Break %: the percentage of opponent service games a player successfully breaks.
• Return Points Won %: the percentage of all points won while returning serve.
• First-Serve Return Points Won: the percentage of return points won against the opponent's first serve.
• Second-Serve Return Points Won: the percentage of return points won against the opponent's second serve.
• Break Points Created per Return Game: the average number of break-point opportunities generated in each
  return game.

Anti-double-counting rule for metrics
Metrics derived from or strongly overlapping with the same information source must be grouped into one evidence family before final
weighting. General Model, Specialist Model, Surface Elo, headline probability, Monte Carlo, and Market may not be assumed
independent. Report each output, but apply a correlation penalty when converting them into evidence strength.

Required derived metrics added to the metrics workflow
For every match where inputs exist, calculate and report: Elo gap; Recent Form gap; Serve/Return gap; headline-vs-MC gap; MC
support bucket (<55 / 55-69 / >=70); Surface Elo support bucket; Specialist support bucket; Matchup Closeness risk tier; number
of independent contradiction families; and leave-one-family-out winner stability.

Interpretation rules
A large number of agreeing metrics is not automatically strong evidence. Reliability, sample, independence, surface relevance, opponent
adjustment, and magnitude determine weight. Likewise, a single disagreement is not automatically a trap. The metrics layer must identify
which evidence is independent and which is duplicative.

Recalibration rule
Every time verified results are added, recompute these empirical bucket records from the newest sample. Never freeze the percentages
above as permanent thresholds. If a bucket has a small sample, label it SUGGESTIVE / TOO SMALL rather than treating it as a rule.

4. Combined Efficiency

• Dominance Ratio: the ratio of a player's return points won percentage to their opponent's return points won
  percentage.
• Opponent-Adjusted Dominance Ratio: Dominance Ratio recalculated after accounting for the relative strength of
  the opponents faced.
• Total Points Won %: the overall percentage of all points won in the match, combining serve and return.
• Matchup-Specific Expected Hold %: the hold percentage a player would be expected to achieve against this specific
  opponent.
• Matchup-Specific Expected Break %: the break percentage a player would be expected to achieve against this
  specific opponent.
• Expected Hold/Break Differential: the projected gap between expected hold rate and expected break rate for this
  matchup.

5. Recent Form

• Last 5 Match Performance: summary results and underlying stats from the player's five most recent matches.
• Last 10 Match Performance: summary results and underlying stats from the player's ten most recent matches.
• Current Hard-Court Swing: performance specifically within the player's current run of hard-court tournaments.
• Trend Direction: whether recent performance is improving, declining, or stable.
• Recent-Performance Acceleration: the rate at which recent performance is improving or declining, not just the
  direction.
• Quality of Last 3–5 Performances: an assessment of opponent strength and dominance level in the most recent
  handful of matches.
• Average Games/Sets Conceded in Wins: the typical number of games or sets given up during recent victories.
• Straight-Set Control Rate: the percentage of recent wins completed in straight sets.

6. Opponent Quality

• Opponent-Adjusted Strength of Schedule: an assessment of how difficult a player's recent opponents have been,
  weighted by quality.
• Ranking-Adjusted Performance: performance evaluated relative to what would be expected given opponents'
  rankings.
• Performance Against Comparable-Ranked Players: results specifically against opponents of similar ranking to the
  player.
• Performance Against Specific Archetypes: results against particular playing styles (e.g., big servers,
  counterpunchers).
• Bad-Loss Rate: the frequency of losses to significantly lower-ranked or weaker opponents.

7. Common-Opponent Network

• Direct Common Opponents: opponents that both players in the matchup have played.
• Who Beat/Lost to the Same Players: a comparison of results each player achieved against shared opponents.
• Scoreline Comparison: how the scorelines compare between each player's matches against the same common
  opponents.

8. Set Profile

• Set-1 Win Rate: the percentage of matches in which a player wins the first set.
• Set-2 Win Rate: the percentage of matches in which a player wins the second set.
• Set-3/Deciding-Set Win Rate: the percentage of matches in which a player wins the deciding set.
• First-Break Frequency: how often a player achieves the first break of serve in a match.
• Record After Losing Set 1: a player's overall win-loss record in matches where they lost the first set.
• Record After Winning Set 1: a player's overall win-loss record in matches where they won the first set.
• Second-Set Performance After Losing Set 1: how a player performs specifically in the second set after dropping
  the first.
• Deciding-Set Win Rate: the percentage of deciding (final) sets a player wins.
• Break-Back Rate: how often a player who has just been broken immediately breaks back.
• Hold/Return Improvement Set 1 → Sets 2/3: whether a player's hold and return numbers materially improve after
  the first set.

9. Comeback/Pressure Behavior

• Wins From Behind: the frequency with which a player wins matches after trailing.
• Break-Consolidation Rate: the percentage of service games held immediately after breaking the opponent.
• Serving-for-Set Conversion: the percentage of times a player successfully closes out a set while serving for it.
• Serving-for-Match Conversion: the percentage of times a player successfully closes out a match while serving for
  it.
• Pressure-Point Performance: how a player performs specifically on break points, set points, and match points
  combined.
• Clutch Hold/Break Performance: hold and break rates measured specifically in high-leverage game situations.
• Tiebreak Record: a player's overall win-loss record in tiebreaks.

10. Straight-Set / 2–0 Metrics

• Straight-Set Win Rate: the percentage of matches a player wins without dropping a set.
• Straight-Set Rate vs Comparable Opposition: straight-set win rate calculated only against opponents of similar
  strength.
• Monte Carlo Straight-Set Probability (Both Players): a simulation-based estimate of each player's probability of
  winning in straight sets.

11. Volatility/Floor

• Performance Variance: how much a player's match-to-match performance level fluctuates.

12. Fatigue/Workload

• Matches in 7 Days: the number of matches a player has completed in the past week.
• Actual Minutes on Court: total time spent playing across recent matches.
• Sets/Games Played (Recent): the total number of sets and games completed in recent matches.

• Late Finishes: matches that concluded unusually late at night, potentially affecting recovery.
• Rest Hours: the amount of time between a player's most recent match and the upcoming one.
• Qualifying Workload: additional matches played in qualifying rounds before the main draw.
• Recent Travel Distance/Time Zones: the distance and time-zone shift involved in a player's recent travel.

13. Availability

• Injuries: any current or recent injury affecting a player's availability or performance.
• Recent Withdrawals: tournaments a player has pulled out of recently.
• Retirements: matches a player has failed to finish due to injury or illness.
• Medical Timeouts: recent instances of on-court medical treatment.
• Layoffs: extended periods away from competition.
• Return-From-Injury Form: performance level specifically in matches played shortly after returning from injury.

14. Ranking Context

• Current Ranking: the player's present official ranking.
• Ranking Trajectory: the recent direction of movement in a player's ranking.
• Rapid Riser/Faller Status: whether a player is moving unusually quickly up or down the rankings.
• Ranking–Performance Disconnect: the gap between a player's current ranking and what their underlying
  performance suggests it should be.

15. Market Layer

• Sportsbook Moneyline Consensus: the average implied win probability across multiple sportsbooks.
• Multiple-Book Comparison: a side-by-side comparison of odds offered across different sportsbooks.
• Opening Price vs Current/Closing Price: how much the betting line has moved from open to close.
• Market Movement: the direction and magnitude of line movement leading up to the match.
• No-Vig Implied Probability: the market's implied win probability after removing the bookmaker's margin.
• Model-vs-Market Divergence: the gap between the model's probability and the market's implied probability.
• Prediction-Market Consensus: implied probabilities drawn from prediction markets rather than sportsbooks.

17. Shot & Rally Metrics

• Forehand/Backhand Effectiveness: the relative effectiveness of each groundstroke wing, and how well opponents
  exploit the weaker one.
• Net-Point Frequency and Success: how often a player comes to net and their win rate when doing so.

• Break-Point Quality: whether break-point opportunities came from sustained return dominance or isolated
  opponent errors, rather than raw conversion rate.
• Hold Vulnerability: how often a player's service games reach 30-30, deuce, or break point even when they

• Return-Game Threat Consistency: the percentage of a player's return games that reach 30-30, deuce, or break

• Opponent Comeback Susceptibility: how prone the opponent is to allowing comebacks after building a lead.

21. Surface & Environmental Context

• Surface-Transition Performance: results in the first one to three matches after switching surfaces.
• Tournament-Round Performance: results broken down by round (qualifying, R1, R2, QF, SF/F).
• Home-Country/Home-Continent Effects: statistically justified performance differences when playing near home.
• Altitude Sensitivity: how a player's performance changes at high altitude.
• Ball/Court-Speed Interaction: how a player's serve and return style interacts with different ball and court speeds.
• Weather Sensitivity: historical performance in heat, humidity, and wind based on actual match data.
• Age/Development Curve: a player's expected performance trajectory based on age and recent improvement rate.
• Inactivity Decay: expected performance loss after specific lengths of layoff.
• Schedule Density (14/30 Days): match load measured over two-week and one-month windows rather than just one
  week.
• Opponent Retirement-Adjusted Records: win totals adjusted so incomplete wins via opponent retirement don't
  inflate perceived form.
• Match-Quality-Adjusted Straight-Set Rate: straight-set win rate adjusted for the quality of opposition faced.
• Expected-Games vs Actual-Games Dominance: comparing expected games won to actual games won to detect
  unsustainable clutch results.
• Luck/Regression Indicators: unusually high tiebreak, break-point, or deciding-set success rates likely to regress
  toward the mean.
• Bayesian Uncertainty/Confidence Intervals: confidence ranges applied to every key statistic so small-sample
  numbers aren't treated the same as large-sample numbers.
• Data-Source Agreement: comparing independent data providers and flagging discrepancies in identity, surface,
  score, ranking, or statistics.

• Sports-Psychologist Presence: whether a player currently works with a mental-game coach.
• Short-Notice Draw Entry: lucky-loser or late-alternate entry status, meaning minimal specific preparation time for

• Fixed Start-Time vs "Not-Before" Uncertainty: whether an unpredictable start time following a long prior match

72. Matchup Nuance

• One-Handed vs Two-Handed Backhand Matchup Effectiveness: how a player's backhand style specifically

• Reach/Wingspan Differential: how height and reach advantages affect net coverage and passing-shot vulnerability,

• Junior/ITF-Era Head-to-Head: earlier meetings between the two players before either reached tour level, if any
  exist.

• Player Public-Statement Sentiment: the tone of pre-match interviews or press conferences (confidence versus

80. Common-Opponent & Opponent-Caliber Metrics

• Common-Opponent Divergent Outcome: cases where Player A beat a specific opponent but Player B lost to that
  same opponent, used as a direct proxy comparison between the two.
• Opponent-Caliber Performance Gap: the gap between a player's best win (or closest near-upset loss) relative to
  their own level, compared against the gap between their worst loss relative to their own level — a ceiling-vs-floor
  opponent-quality spread.

81. Further Differentiating Metrics

• Locker-Room/Backstage Conflict History: documented disputes or confrontations involving the player that could
  affect focus.
• Anthem/Opening-Ceremony Delay Effect: performance impact from pre-match ceremonies, such as in Davis Cup
  or opening-night matches.
• Featured/Center-Court Exposure Rate: how often a player is scheduled on the main show court versus outer
  courts, and whether performance differs with visibility.
• Rain-Delay Resumption Performance: how a player performs after a match is halted mid-play and resumed.
• Overnight-Suspension Resumption Performance: performance the following day after a match is suspended
  overnight and completed later.
• Late-Opponent-Substitution Adjustment: performance when the scheduled opponent withdraws and is replaced
  by a lucky loser or alternate on short notice.
• Weekday vs Weekend Performance Split: whether results differ playing on weekdays versus weekends.
• Consecutive-Day-Play Penalty: performance drop from playing back-to-back days with no rest day between
  rounds.
• Training-Base Relocation: a recent change in home training academy or base city that could disrupt routine.
• Prior Withdrawal Pattern at This Event: whether the player has withdrawn from this specific tournament in past
  years.
• Electronic-Line-Calling Adjustment Lag: how recently a player has played events using fully electronic line
  calling, and any adaptation period involved.
• First-Week vs Second-Week Major Split: whether a player's form historically differs between the first and second
  week of a Grand Slam.
• Prior-Year Round Reached at This Exact Event: how far the player advanced at this event last year, as a comfort
  and confidence marker distinct from raw stats.
• Support-Staff Turnover (Stringer/Physio): recent changes to non-coaching staff that could affect routine or

• Travel-Friction Reports: visa issues, long transit, or missed connections reported en route to the event.
• Home-Climate Differential: how much hotter, colder, or drier the event is compared to the player's home training
  climate, distinct from generic heat/humidity sensitivity.
